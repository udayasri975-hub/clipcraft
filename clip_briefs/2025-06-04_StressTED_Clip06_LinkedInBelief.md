## Transcript / Quote
> "A study tracked 30,000 adults in the US over 8 years. People who experienced a lot of stress had a 43% increased risk of dying. BUT — that was only true for people who *also believed* that stress was harming their health. People who experienced a lot of stress but did NOT view stress as harmful had the *lowest* risk of death in the entire study — lower than people who had very little stress."

## Editorial Notes
- **Hook:** "The most dangerous thing about stress isn't the stress itself. It's what you believe about it."
- **Key framing:** This isn't about eliminating stress — it's about changing your relationship with it. Practical, science-backed reframe for leaders and professionals.
- **Tone direction:** Professional, science-informed, contrarian. "Here's what the data actually says" energy.

## LinkedIn Post (Full Draft)

> We've been told stress is the enemy.
>
> Reduce it. Manage it. Eliminate it.
>
> But what if the science says the opposite?
>
> 🧠 A study tracked 30,000 adults over 8 years. People under high stress had a 43% higher risk of dying — but ONLY if they believed stress was harming them.
>
> People who experienced high stress AND didn't see it as harmful had the *lowest* risk of death in the entire study — lower than people with almost no stress at all.
>
> Think about that for a second.
>
> The same amount of stress. Two completely different health outcomes. The only variable? Whether you viewed the stress as helpful or harmful.
>
> This changes everything about how we should think about workplace pressure, high-stakes projects, and ambitious goals.
>
> The goal isn't less stress. The goal is a different relationship with it.
>
> What's one stressful situation you've faced that ultimately made you stronger?
>
